MVP

MOST VALUABLE PLAYER

SGA didn't just repeat as MVP — he made his case as one of the most efficient scoring guards the NBA has ever seen.

The Thunder went 64–18, finishing with the best record in the league. SGA averaged 31.1 points, 4.3 rebounds and 6.6 assists per game while shooting 55.3% from the field, 38.6% from three and 87.9% from the free-throw line

The biggest difference between SGA and the other MVP candidates was how consistently he delivered without needing an overwhelming amount of possessions.

He finished second in scoring behind Luka Dončić, but his shooting efficiency was extraordinary. He became the first guard in NBA history to average at least 30 points while shooting 55% or better from the field.

His ability to get into the paint, draw fouls, hit midrange shots and control the pace of the game made him one of the hardest players in the league to defend.

SGA's 2025–26 season was a historic individual campaign. He became a back-to-back MVP, joined the exclusive group of players to win consecutive awards, and helped establish Oklahoma City as the NBA's dominant regular-season team.

The one major caveat: the Thunder's season ultimately ended in the Western Conference Finals, where they lost a seven-game series to Victor Wembanyama's San Antonio Spurs.
